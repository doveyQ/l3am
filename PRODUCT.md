# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Astro + Tailwind CSS + TypeScript (user's explicit choice, not delegated). Greenfield — nothing is scaffolded yet.

## Users

Primary: B2B decision-makers in the DACH market — KMU owners, Geschäftsführer, and solo entrepreneurs (EPU), plus CTOs, founders, and innovation leads.

Their situation: they run a business carrying repetitive manual operations and/or an inadequate web presence, and they are evaluating whether an outside engineering partner is worth the spend. They arrive skeptical of agencies and of AI marketing language.

What they want: clear ROI, evidence of engineering reliability, and zero buzzwords or technical confusion. Jargon and unsubstantiated AI claims actively cost trust with this audience.

## Product Purpose

leam (l3am.dev) is an engineering studio. It exists to give KMUs and EPUs access to genuine software engineering rigor — the kind normally reserved for larger budgets — without agency overhead.

Two service lines:

1. **Web engineering** — from high-end, Awwwards-level frontends and interactive websites through to complete full-stack web applications with custom backend architectures.
2. **AI automation** — bespoke workflows that eliminate repetitive manual business operations.

Success for this site: a qualified DACH business owner understands what leam builds, believes the studio can actually deliver it, and makes contact.

## Positioning

Deep academic and practical software engineering rigor, without agency overhead.

Founded by three TU Graz Software Engineering students. The claim a neighboring agency could not truthfully copy is the combination: formal software engineering depth (architecture, full-stack systems, correctness) paired with design execution at Awwwards level, delivered directly by the engineers rather than through an agency layer of account and project management.

Note the tension this site must resolve rather than paper over: the same rigor argument makes the studio credible and makes its youth visible. The founder credentials are the asset, not something to hide behind.

## Operating Context

- Market and language: DACH. **The site ships in German only.** All copy, labels, dates, numbers, and currency in German conventions. No i18n scaffolding required.
- Audience vocabulary: KMU, EPU, Geschäftsführer. Address form (Sie vs. du) is an open decision — see Capabilities and Constraints.
- Evaluation happens on desktop during a workday and on mobile; both are primary, neither is a fallback.
- The visitor is comparing leam against local agencies, freelancers, and doing nothing.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Domain: l3am.dev. Brand name is written **leam** (lowercase in the wordmark as given).
- Team size: three founders. Capacity claims must stay consistent with a three-person studio.
- The site itself is a portfolio piece: it is direct evidence of the "Awwwards-level frontend" claim. A conventional agency template would falsify the positioning.
- **Undecided / not yet established** (do not invent):
  - Address form: formal *Sie* or informal *du*.
  - Pricing, packages, and rates.
  - Service-level, availability, or delivery-time commitments.
  - Legal entity form, business registration, and Impressum/Datenschutz content (legally required for an Austrian/DACH site — content must come from the user).
  - Contact mechanism (form, email, booking link).

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **leam**, at **l3am.dev**.
- Self-description: "engineering studio" — not "agency". The distinction is the positioning.
- Voice: clear, concrete, no buzzwords, no technical confusion. This is a stated audience requirement, not a style preference.
- No logo, wordmark, color, or typographic assets have been supplied yet.

## Evidence on Hand

- **Named client work exists** — real projects the studio may reference by client name and logo. The specific projects, names, logos, screenshots, and outcome metrics have **not yet been supplied** and must come from the user before any case-study or logo-wall surface can be built.
- **Founder credentials are real and citable**: three TU Graz Software Engineering students/founders. Individual names, photos, and specifics not yet supplied.
- **Absent — must not be fabricated**: testimonials, quotes, client counts, revenue or ROI figures, time-saved percentages, award wins or Awwwards recognition, press mentions, certifications, and years-in-business claims. "Awwwards-level" describes the standard of frontend work leam produces; it is not a claim of an award received, and must never be rendered as one.

## Product Principles

1. **Demonstrate, don't assert.** The audience discounts claims about engineering quality; the site's own build quality is the argument. Every claim of capability should have a corresponding thing the visitor can see or feel.
2. **Plain German over vocabulary.** Buzzword avoidance is a hard requirement from the audience, especially around AI. Name the business outcome, not the technology.
3. **Engineering rigor is the through-line.** Both service lines — web and AI automation — are the same underlying claim. The site should read as one studio with one competence, not two offerings bolted together.
4. **Credentials carry the trust load.** With a young three-person studio and no testimonials, TU Graz pedigree plus real named client work are the credibility mechanism. Place them where skepticism actually peaks.
5. **Respect the decision-maker's time.** A Geschäftsführer evaluating mid-workday needs the offer, the proof, and the next step legible fast — expressiveness must not cost clarity.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific accessibility requirement was established in the interview. Default to WCAG 2.2 AA as the baseline for a public B2B site.
